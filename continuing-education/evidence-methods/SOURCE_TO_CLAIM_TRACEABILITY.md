# Source-to-Claim Traceability

## Purpose

Source-to-claim traceability is the ability to follow an educational teaching point back to the evidence needed to support the exact proposition being presented.

The purpose is to prevent a common failure in medical education:

> A citation is present, but the cited source does not fully support the statement.

---

## Core Traceability Pathway

**Claim → Source → Evidence Type → Strength and Limitations → Interpretation → Educational Application**

For compound statements, use the more granular pathway:

**Source → Exact Proposition → Evidence Type → Strength and Limitations → Interpretation → Educational Conclusion**

---

## 1. Identify the Exact Proposition

Start with the smallest meaningful factual statement that requires external verification.

Example:

> Treatment X improves disease control, reduces hospitalization, improves quality of life, and is recommended as first-line therapy.

This is not one claim.

It contains at least four:

1. Treatment X improves disease control.
2. Treatment X reduces hospitalization.
3. Treatment X improves quality of life.
4. Treatment X is recommended as first-line therapy.

Each claim may require different evidence.

---

## 2. Decompose Compound Claims

Decompose when:

- a sentence contains multiple outcomes;
- efficacy and safety are combined;
- observational and causal statements are mixed;
- an evidence finding and a guideline recommendation appear in the same sentence;
- clinical benefit and healthcare utilization are combined;
- one citation would otherwise appear to support several independent propositions.

Do not decompose merely for stylistic reasons.

The objective is evidentiary clarity.

---

## 3. Define the Evidence Requirement

For each atomic claim, identify:

- population;
- intervention or exposure;
- comparator;
- outcome;
- setting;
- timeframe;
- evidence type required.

Example:

**Claim:** Treatment X reduces hospitalization in adults with condition Y.

Evidence must support:

- Treatment X;
- adult population with condition Y;
- hospitalization as an outcome;
- the direction and magnitude of effect represented in the educational wording.

A source showing biomarker improvement does not by itself establish reduced hospitalization.

---

## 4. Evaluate Entailment

A source entails a claim when the source reasonably supports the proposition without requiring an unacknowledged inferential leap.

### Direct

The source directly evaluates or establishes the proposition.

### Partially Direct

The source supports one component but not the entire statement.

### Indirect

The proposition depends on extrapolation or synthesis.

### Unsupported

The source does not adequately support the proposition.

---

## 5. Protect Against Citation Proximity

A citation placed at the end of a paragraph can create the appearance that it supports every preceding assertion.

Do not rely on citation placement alone to communicate evidentiary scope.

When necessary:

- separate claims into sentences;
- place `[REF]` beside the proposition requiring support;
- use multiple references;
- distinguish recommendations from findings;
- label interpretation or inference.

---

## 6. Protect Against Narrative Corroboration Spillover

Evidence supporting one component of a narrative does not automatically support other components.

For example:

Evidence may establish:

> Treatment X reduces symptom score.

It does not automatically establish:

> Treatment X prevents long-term complications.

Even when both propositions fit a coherent mechanism or clinical narrative, each requires its own evidentiary basis.

---

## 7. Trace Educational Conclusions Backward

For each major teaching point, test the chain in reverse:

**Educational conclusion**
↓
**Interpretation**
↓
**Evidence strength and limitations**
↓
**Evidence type**
↓
**Exact proposition**
↓
**Source**

If any link is unclear, the teaching point requires revision or further verification.

---

## 8. Traceability Table

Recommended review format:

| ID | Exact Claim | Evidence Needed | Proposed Source | Evidence Type | Support Level | Limitations | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Exact proposition | Type required | Source | RCT/guideline/etc. | Direct/partial/indirect | Key limitations | Verified / revise |

---

## 9. Review Questions

For every major claim ask:

1. What exactly is being claimed?
2. Does the sentence contain multiple claims?
3. What evidence would support each proposition?
4. Does the cited source support the whole proposition?
5. Is the population the same?
6. Is the outcome the same?
7. Is the claim stronger than the evidence?
8. Has interpretation been presented as a source finding?
9. Has a recommendation been presented as a fact?
10. Has evidence supporting one component spilled over into the rest of the narrative?

---

## Core Rule

**One citation near a statement does not prove that the statement is evidence-supported.**

Traceability requires proposition-level support.
